Groups of AI agents underuse resources despite shared evidence of increased capacity

Takahiro Ezaki, Naoto Imura, Katsuhiro Nishinari
arXiv arXiv cs.MA2026-11-17 LLM & agentic AI

LLM agents sharing limited server capacity and status information under deadlines, a multi-agent resource allocation setting relevant to agentic operational decisions.

Abstract

When AI agents share limited resources, such as computing services or public infrastructure, their individual choices affect how much work they complete as a group. Can sharing evidence of what is available let a group make full use of its resources after capacity changes? We tested this with language-model agents choosing between two simulated servers. One server recovered while its status report still listed it as unavailable. Each server had limited capacity per round, so rejected jobs required another attempt before the deadline. Telling agents that recovery was possible reduced missed deadlines. In controlled tests with Anthropic's Claude Haiku 5.5, no agent tried the recovered server after a rule reminder, yet all chose it when shown a successful request record. After sharing, all five models tested left capacity unused. Keeping the success record fixed, we gave agents numbers without instructions, using each value equally often but giving group members either identical or distinct numbers. With OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol or Google's Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, distinct numbers spread simultaneous requests across servers and increased job completions even though overall choice frequencies barely changed. Telling AI agents which resources are available leaves a second design challenge: helping them spread demand across those resources.

Optimal Energy and LLM Task Scheduling for Multiple Cloud Data Centers

Zhongqi Zhao, Lei Fan, Zhu Han
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-17 Transport-grid integration

MILP scheduling of LLM training tasks with data center energy and facility operation; a close optimisation-plus-energy formulation, though not transport.

Abstract

This paper studies coordinated Large Language Model (LLM) training-task scheduling and facility operation for geographically distributed cloud data centers (CDCs). The central question is how two scheduling flexibilities - cross-CDC task transfer and task reordering - affect service and economic outcomes when remote execution remains subject to compute, bandwidth, energy, and zone-temperature constraints. We formulate a mixed-integer optimization model that jointly determines task outcomes, heterogeneous-node assignment, execution timing, pre-execution data transfer, node activation, and facility operation. A deterministic 24-hour case study contains 3 CDCs, 15 heterogeneous graphics processing unit (GPU) nodes, 4 thermal zones per CDC, and 103 tasks. In this instance, enabling transfer increases net profit by 57.9% relative to the otherwise matched no-transfer variant and increases completed tasks from 76 to 103. The more restrictive no-transfer FIFO-prefix variant completes 14 tasks and yields negative profit. The proposed schedule transfers 39 tasks, achieves 70.3% node utilization, and maintains zone temperatures within 24°C-27°C. These fixed-instance results indicate that cross-CDC transfer and task-reordering flexibility can improve the modeled profit and task-completion outcomes under the studied conditions.

When Do Typed Decision Models Work in Transportation? Evidence from Travel Choice and Traffic Signal Control

Hongyang Zhang, Boyuan Deng, Siyuan Feng, Kai Chen et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.CL2026-11-17 LLM & agentic AI

Evaluates typed LLM decision models for travel mode choice and traffic signal control; directly transport decision-making with RL baselines.

Abstract

Typed decision models combine natural-language understanding with a structured interface that returns a selected action and an option-level probability distribution without generating lengthy textual responses. Their low inference cost and response latency make them attractive for transportation problems with predefined alternatives. This paper investigates their effectiveness, robustness, and efficiency in two representative transportation tasks: individual travel-mode choice prediction and sequential traffic signal control. We systematically evaluate TypeSafe Jev and GPT-6 Luna Decisions against conventional transportation methods, reinforcement learning algorithms, and four chat-based large language models. Experiments on the Swissmetro dataset show that typed decision models achieve lower prediction accuracy than traditional discrete choice models and the strongest chat-based baseline, while their performance varies substantially with input representation and question framing. In closed-loop traffic signal control experiments, typed decision models demonstrate competitive performance against established controllers in several scenarios, but exhibit performance degradation under more challenging network conditions. Furthermore, the typed models achieve lower inference latency and computational cost than the evaluated chat-based large language models, highlighting their potential for efficient, zero-shot transportation decision-making. Overall, these findings demonstrate both the potential and limitations of typed decision models, emphasizing the importance of input design, task characteristics, and robustness evaluation for their effective application in transportation systems.

CrossEnv: Synthesizing Verifiable Cross-Environment Tasks for Long-Horizon Agent Training

Zhengfeng Yu, Tian Lan, Zhijing Wu, Enbo Zhao et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-17 LLM & agentic AI

Synthesizes verifiable cross-environment tool-use tasks for training long-horizon LLM agents; agentic but not about optimisation or transport.

Abstract

Executable synthetic environments provide scalable training tasks and verifiable feedback for long-horizon agents. Existing environment synthesis methods primarily generate tasks within individual environments. However, these tasks do not adequately capture real-world interactions across services, where actions in one service depend on information obtained from another. Synthesizing such tasks is challenging because data generated across environments must remain consistent to support executable and verifiable interactions. To address this challenge, we introduce CrossEnv, a framework that uses an entity-tool graph to identify connections between environments and aligns task-specific data to synthesize executable, verifiable cross-environment tasks. We further propose CrossEnv-Curriculum, which progresses from single-environment tool use to coordination across more environments and business domains, before introducing user interaction. Across four external benchmarks, combining cross-environment training data with this curriculum achieves competitive performance with fewer training examples. Further analyses support the benefits of cross-environment data and curriculum training, and provide evidence of transfer to new environment combinations, external tasks less similar to the training data, and longer workflows.

Harnesses That Improve Themselves: A Unifying Framework for Self-Improving Agent Harnesses

Deep Shah, Nehal Kathrotia
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-17 LLM & agentic AI

Self-improving agent harnesses that diagnose and patch their own runtime; relevant agent engineering background with evaluation of reliability.

Abstract

Whether a foundation model succeeds as an autonomous agent depends as much on its runtime software as on its weights. This runtime, the agent harness, manages context and memory, steers multi-turn control, executes tool actions in sandboxes, and verifies work before stopping. Because models differ in how they use tools and how they fail, a static harness tuned for one model or task often stalls, wastes tokens, or regresses on another. This bottleneck has driven a shift from prompt tuning to self-improving agent harnesses: systems that inspect their own execution traces, separate runtime bugs from model limits, and rewrite their own code. We present a unifying architectural and design framework for self-improving agent harnesses. The framework pairs four editable runtime components (context and state, agent control, action interfaces, and verification and recovery) with a four-stage loop that diagnoses failures, proposes code patches, validates candidates, and deploys updates. Using this framework, we show how optimizers trace errors across long trajectories and discover reusable runtime mechanisms such as pre-stop verifiers, loop breakers, and persistent memory. We further show why full-harness rewrites overfit or break across modules, and how modular updates with validation gates generalize across models while bounding token cost. To keep self-modification safe, we establish practical defenses against recurring failure modes including prompt bloat, benchmark gaming, and evaluator tampering. Finally, experiments on SWE-bench Lite validate this framework, showing that gated modular evolution outperforms prompt reflection and full-harness rewrites across models and repositories at a fraction of the token cost.

PVTSizing 2.0: A Fast and Reliable Analog Circuit Sizing Operator for Agentic EDA Workflows

Zichen Kong, Minghuan Song, Xiyuan Tang, Ziming Wei et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AR2026-11-17 LLM & agentic AI

LLM-adviser plus numerical optimizer for analog circuit sizing across PVT corners; an agentic human-in-loop optimisation workflow, though in a different domain.

Abstract

Analog circuit design automation requires both coordinated tool execution and efficient numerical search. Large language models (LLMs) offer new ways to organize tool execution, but finding one sizing that satisfies specifications across process, voltage, and temperature (PVT) corners remains costly. PVTSizing 2.0 addresses these needs through a human-agent workflow built around an independently usable numerical optimizer. An agent carries the designer's approved task through execution and result review. The optimizer reduces unnecessary training through loss-guided updates and uses critic-guided local refinement to explore additional candidates while accounting for cross-corner conflicts. An optional LLM adviser supplies parameter relationships to focus numerical search on circuit-informed directions, while the optimizer selects adjustment amplitudes and verifies their effects through simulation. Across four circuit benchmarks, the standalone optimizer completes all 40 runs and achieves a 7.3x speedup in overall mean runtime over the original PVTSizing, with a comparable mean sample count. In a separate study, the best tested knowledge-injection configuration further reduces mean runtime by 39.9% and simulation samples by 35.4% relative to the standalone optimizer.

Pareto-Informed Smart "Predict, then Optimize" for Multi-Objective Combinatorial Problems

Aabhash Dhakal, Tim Lachner
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-11-17 AI for optimisation

Decision-focused learning with SPO+ for multi-objective combinatorial problems; directly on predict-then-optimise methods the researcher would value.

Abstract

Many real-world planning applications, from logistics, routing to resource allocation, give rise to optimization problems that need to balance multiple, conflicting objectives. While machine learning is often used to predict the unknown parameters or costs of these problems, standard models trained on prediction accuracy can lead to suboptimal plans. Decision-Focused Learning (DFL) addresses this by integrating the downstream optimization task into the training objective. However, existing DFL methods for multi-objective problems rely on simple scalarization, which fails to capture the rich trade-offs of the Pareto Frontier. To address this gap, we propose a Pareto-aware extension to single-objective Smart "Predict, then Optimize" (SPO+) for multi-objective combinatorial optimization problems. Our loss function leverages the true Pareto Frontier, forming a target decision that aligns with a sampled preference vector, and minimizes regret against this target. Experiments on multi-objective shortest path and multi-objective knapsack problems show that our approach outperforms traditional two-stage Mean Squared Error (MSE) baselines. It also surpasses scalarization-based SPO+ approaches.

Think Once, Act Fast: Cost-Effective Recursive Self-Improvement for Decision Model Harness

Siqi Lai, Haitao Li, Hao Liu
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-17 LLM & agentic AI

System-1/System-2 agent framework with recursive harness improvement for decision making; agentic decision support relevant to operational decisions.

Abstract

Fast System-1 models enable responsive actions but struggle with complex or unfamiliar tasks. Large language model (LLM)-based System-2 provides strong reasoning capabilities, yet repeated invocation incurs substantial latency and computational costs. To bridge this gap, we propose JevRSI, a System-1/System-2 collaboration framework that transforms LLM reasoning into transferable decision support through recursive harness improvement. A Jev-style System-1 model enables low-latency action selection, while an asynchronous System-2 LLM develops a modular harness. The harness integrates procedures, state memory, and world models to provide decision contexts spanning the past, present, and future. Utility-driven retrieval selects modules based on both contextual relevance and their utility on previous tasks. Environmental feedback guides targeted revisions and enables recursive self-improvement. Experiments across three datasets show that JevRSI consistently improves decision quality, matching or surpassing LLM agents in several settings. It preserves low-latency execution with favorable performance-cost trade-offs. Moreover, System-2 development interventions decrease as the harness evolves, amortizing System-2 costs through continuous improvement. Our project is available at https://github.com/usail-hkust/JevRSI.
