Probabilistic Power Estimation and MPC-based Scheduling of Construction Electric Vehicles Supported by Mobile Charging Stations

Shubhan Mital, Avik Ghosh, Daniela Rojas, Sujit Dey et al.
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-16 E-bus scheduling & charging

MILP scheduling of construction EVs with mobile charging stations under MPC with uncertainty, directly matching electrified fleet scheduling and charging optimisation.

Abstract

The adoption of construction electric vehicles (CEVs) is held back by sparse on-site charging infrastructure, limited mobility, and insufficient knowledge of their power consumption. Mobile charging stations (MCSs) towed between grid connection points and construction sites address the first barrier, yet scheduling them optimally requires an accurate estimate of the CEV power consumption. In this paper, we address these gaps, first, by estimating the power of each subactivity of a representative CEV (a compact electric excavator) based on real-world construction demonstration data. We use Bayesian linear regression to obtain the subactivity power distributions, accounting for their operational uncertainty. Second, we formulate a mixed-integer program that jointly schedules CEV work and charging together with MCS routing, charging, and discharging, optimizing electricity costs, carbon emissions, work completion, and MCS travel. We embed the optimizer in a shrinking-horizon model predictive control (MPC) loop to perform real-time control on the CEV-MCS system. Two MPC variants, one a certainty-equivalent MPC (CE-MPC) controller, and the other a scenario-based MPC (SB-MPC) controller, are compared in closed-loop against an open-loop day-ahead schedule. Realistic simulations show that SB-MPC performs the best, lowering the operating costs by $60.2\%$ relative to the open-loop baseline, and by $23.3\%$ relative to CE-MPC, demonstrating the efficacy of incorporating predictive control with feedback. The code is available at https://github.com/Shubhanflash22/CEV-MCS-MPC-Joint-Scheduling

Grid-Responsive Pricing of Dynamic Wireless Charging with Coupled Residential Demand

Zhiheng Wang, Dipanjan Ghose, S. Sivaranjani, Junjie Qin et al.
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-17 Transport-grid integration

Jointly prices dynamic wireless charging for EVs with residential charging displacement and feeder loading via MPC; directly on transport-grid integration.

Abstract

Dynamic wireless charging (DWC) is an emerging technology that enables electric vehicles (EVs) to recharge while traveling. Its unique operational model creates two revenue streams for DWC operators through lane access tolls and electricity sales. However, these prices influence not only traffic flow and roadway energy delivery but also how much EV charging moves to homes instead, potentially within the same distribution network. Neglecting this coupling can underestimate grid loading and compromise pricing decisions. We develop a coupled traffic, EV energy, driver-response, and AC distribution network model with a model predictive control (MPC) framework that jointly optimizes DWC tolls and charging prices while explicitly accounting for displaced residential charging and network losses. Using 78 days of I-210 West traffic data, we show that conventional MPC exceeds feeder loading limits on 52 days, whereas the proposed residential load aware controller eliminates these violations with only 0.6% lower operator revenue. Under a more constrained feeder configuration, it eliminates 50 overloaded days with a 0.9% revenue reduction. Sensitivity analyses highlight the importance of accurate residential demand estimation and feeder loss modeling, while peak-aware pricing further reduces daily peak loading by 3.7% relative to the base load. These results demonstrate the value of coordinated pricing that captures the interaction between roadway and residential charging.

Agent-Guided Configuration Tuning for SMT Solvers

Cong Wang, Qiaoyi Zheng, Zhaoguo Wang, Fan Long
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-16 LLM & agentic AI

LLM agents tuning solver configurations with experiment records and a deterministic harness; agents running optimisation pipelines.

Abstract

SMT solvers expose many configuration choices that determine how well their existing algorithms perform on a workload. We present, to our knowledge, the first workflow that uses general-purpose LLM agents to tune SMT solver configurations. Agents read solver documentation, source code, and persistent experiment records to propose option changes, and a deterministic harness measures each proposal, validates the returned models, and selects changes separately for each competition Division. Over roughly nine weeks, our tuning campaign recorded 4,545 proposals for cvc5 across 13 Divisions and 36 logics. We evaluate the tuned configurations on the 2026 and 2025 SMT-COMP ModelValidation benchmarks under a 24-second cutoff. On problems not used for training, they validate more problems than official cvc5 in ten of the 13 Divisions in 2026 and in eight in 2025, with geometric-mean gains of 7.8% and 6.8% across Divisions. On the complete official selections, which include training problems, the tuned floating-point counts exceed those of every individual competition participant in both years. Further studies show how configuration changes make existing algorithms effective by changing access to strategies, scheduling, and option compatibility, and how benchmark families trade off against one another. The agents' experiment traces contain hypothesis-based proposals in every tuning group, and 64.3% of the extracted hypotheses draw on prior measurements. These results show that agent-guided tuning can specialize a mature SMT solver and that accumulated experimental evidence informs sustained search.

An Explainable Agentic AI Framework for Distribution System Load Restoration

S. M. Monzurul Hoque Chowdhury, Qinghua Li
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-17 Transport-grid integration

Multi-LLM agentic framework for distribution system load restoration with AC power-flow checks; operational decisions in power systems, close to grid and agentic interests.

Abstract

Distribution system load restoration is a sequential decision-making problem in which restoration actions must maximize served load, prioritize critical loads, and preserve power-flow feasibility. This paper proposes an explainable agentic artificial intelligence (AI) framework for distribution system load restoration. The proposed framework uses multiple large language model (LLM) agents for planner guidance, strategic plan generation, decision arbitration, memory-based feedback, and explanation generation. Unlike unconstrained LLM-based control, the proposed method restricts agent decisions to feasible restoration actions and evaluates all candidate plans using deterministic AC power-flow feasibility checks. The framework is evaluated on IEEE 33-bus and 69-bus distribution feeders, as well as a larger IEEE 118-bus test case, and compared against reinforcement learning and planning baselines. The results show that the proposed agentic framework achieves better restoration performance while producing structured explanations for operator-facing decision support. A direct comparison with a Single-LLM-Agent baseline further shows that the proposed multi-agent decomposition improves weighted restored load and critical-load recovery compared with a monolithic LLM decision structure.

Consensus-based Economic Dispatch for Self-organized Local Energy Communities

Dimitrios Lagos, Josh Eichman, Georgios Tsaousoglou
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-15 Transport-grid integration

Decentralised voltage-constrained economic dispatch for local energy communities with learned voltage surrogate; relevant to DER and distribution-network coordination.

Abstract

The increasing penetration of distributed energy resources (DERs) is transforming traditional distribution networks into dynamic, decentralized systems, creating a pressing need for scalable, autonomous coordination mechanisms that do not rely on centralized control or full system observability. In particular, local energy communities need to self-organize and coordinate energy schedules while respecting network constraints, especially voltage limits, despite lacking detailed knowledge of the distribution grid's topology and parameters. Existing distributed optimization approaches either neglect such physical constraints or assume perfect knowledge of the network model. This paper addresses the Voltage-Constrained Economic Dispatch (VCED) problem in a fully decentralized, consensus-based manner, without any coordinating entity. We use sparse-group LASSO regression to learn a linear surrogate of nodal voltages from local power and voltage measurements alone. The surrogate serves a dual purpose: it enters each agent's local problem as a voltage penalty and induces the communication graph over which the agents reach consensus. We provide a theoretical bound on the optimality loss caused by the surrogate voltage estimator inaccuracy. Our numerical results, on an IEEE-33 network, show that the scheme reduces the share of bus-timesteps voltage violations from 30\% to below 3\%, while staying within 1\% of a centralized benchmark computed with full network knowledge. We further quantify how the sparsity of the communication graph affects both the optimality gap and the residual violations.

The Grid's Own Language: A Common Information Model Layer for LLM Grid Assistants

Antonio Alcántara, Ioannis Karampinis, Spyros Chatzivasileiadis
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-15 Transport-grid integration

CIM information layer for LLM grid assistants in power-system operation with solver-verified answers; relevant to grid-facing agentic tools.

Abstract

Assistants built on large language models (LLMs) are entering power system operation. Current designs delegate computations to validated solvers, but the agent still infers the type, role and state of each element from its name and tool-specific formats, and errors propagate to every study. The Common Information Model (CIM), the IEC standard in which operators already exchange network models, defines unambiguously what each element is and how it connects. We propose a CIM layer for LLM grid assistants and implement it in CONDUCTOR, an open-source assistant. Both the agent and its power-system studies read the layer. It labels the provenance of each fact (stated in the data, suggested only by a name, or inferred from topology), loads operators' CGMES models directly, and builds in $\leq$1\,s on the 9,241-bus PEGASE network. Deterministic checks compare every answer with solver results and the layer. On 37 tasks about the CIGRE medium-voltage and IEEE 30-bus networks, the agent gets right 97\,\% of the graded facts with the layer, such as a unit's energy source or a switch's normal state, against 55\,\% without it. We argue that a CIM layer should be a default component of grid assistants.

AI Agents Executing an RMS and EMT Ride-Through Workflow on a 2,000-Bus Synthetic Texas Grid: A Case Study with Open-Source Tools

Tam Kemabonta, Brighton Ombuki, Vladimir Abdelnour
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-17 LLM & agentic AI

AI agents execute a power-grid ride-through simulation workflow with open-source tools; agentic simulation pipeline relevant to the researcher, though in power systems.

Abstract

This paper reports a case study of engineer-supervised AI agents executing a predefined RMS and EMT ride-through workflow on the 2,000-bus ACTIVSg2000 synthetic Texas grid with open-source tools. The test case is a 133 MW battery with a co-located 130 MW data-center load, assessed against ride-through criteria fixed before simulation and for loss of synchronism elsewhere in the grid. One agent ran 44 RMS cases and 15 without-project comparisons in ANDES; the other ran a 220-row EMT intake bench and an 84-row system study in DPsim and ParaEMT on a Kron-reduced area. Across the 44 RMS cases, 127 criterion cells fail, including loss of synchronism in all 44. Two findings concern the project plant. The battery keeps absorbing reactive power during sags on the light-load schedule (B3, as low as -19.8 Mvar on S4 T1), and the data-center load returns only at 5.00-5.56 s after long bolted faults (L3), which misses the ERCOT load-return requirement. In 13 of the 15 comparisons, which the authors selected after slips or speed-gate stops, the same machines slip without the project; the other 29 runs have no without-project comparison. RMS and EMT values differ in network, plant model, controls, operating point, and base, and so they are reported as separate, unaligned observations. The paper reports the errors the authors identified in the agent logs, 63 author messages, and 3.8 days from agent start to the integrated study report. The agents executed and documented the predefined ride-through and stability workflow, which covers one part of an interconnection study.

ATLAS-Finance: Benchmarking AI Agents in Realistic Banking Environments

Elaine Lau, Antoine Pangas, Alexandre Finot, Collin Schweiker et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-17 LLM & agentic AI

Benchmarks frontier agents on multi-step financial modelling across tool environments; relevant as an agent evaluation study, though not transport or optimisation.

Abstract

Professional work involves under-specified assignments, where relevant information is spread across workplace applications and may conflict or change over time. To evaluate how well frontier agents can perform economically valuable work in such settings, we introduce ATLAS-Finance, a benchmark of 100 end-to-end investment-banking tasks across 13 simulated firms. As in real banking workflows, agents must search email, chat, calendars, notes, and files to identify requirements, gather information, reconcile conflicts/updates, and then build complex financial models. Tasks were developed by experienced bankers based on their real deal work and take them 15-30 hours to complete. Agent deliverables are evaluated with expert-authored rubrics (median: 124.5 criteria) covering financial rationale and results, spreadsheet construction, completeness, and formatting. Despite completing substantial portions of the requested work, current agents rarely produce correct deliverables. Across today's frontier models, the best ones (Claude Opus and Fable) pass fewer than 11% of tasks.

DiscoveryAgent: A Framework for Scientist-centric Agentic AI

Owen Queen, Ioana Ciuca, Emma Dann, Jean Somalwar et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-16 LLM & agentic AI

Scientist-steered agentic research framework with audit agents; relevant agent oversight design, though not optimisation or transport.

Abstract

AI agents can assist scientific research, but balancing autonomous investigation with scientists' ability to inspect and redirect ongoing work remains a central design challenge. We present DiscoveryAgent, a domain-agnostic, scientist-centric system that combines autonomous investigation with scientist-directed hypothesis steering. Scientists select, refine, or replace hypotheses between research rounds, while audit agents interrogate checkpointed records to trace claims, reproduce analyses, and inform subsequent decisions. Across two predictive modeling tasks, DiscoveryAgent produced the best variable-star classifier among evaluated baselines and, with scientist steering, a protein variant-effect predictor surpassed by only one of 106 ProteinGym baselines. We develop a rigorous checkpointing system that enables highly capable audit agents, a scalable form of oversight for agentic research. Audit agents autonomously identify errors at higher rates than previous AI scientist agents, and scientists collaborating with audit agents identify 14x more valid errors versus scientists performing manual review. In three case studies led by domain experts across physical science, social science, and life science, we developed a multimodal photometric-redshift estimator that resolves complementary failures of images and photometry, achieving 0.971 AUROC for low-redshift classification; documented an 83% rise in Florida homeowners-insurance premiums from 2013 to 2023, with a growing, regressive burden; and identified CD4+ T-cell relay genes, which both receive and exert broad transcriptional regulation, enriched for autoimmune disease associations. Together, these results show how autonomous research can expand scientists' empirical reach while centering their authority over research direction and their ability to understand and challenge the evidence.

GRACE: Parallel Best-of-N Multi-Agent Analysis of Closed-Form Questions over Dataframes with Guardrail Selection and Adaptive Retry

Fabio Picariello, Salvatore Cipolla, Susanna Bonura, Vito Morreale
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-16 LLM & agentic AI

Parallel best-of-N multi-agent code generation with guardrail selection and adaptive retry for dataframe analysis; relevant agent selection design.

Abstract

LLM agents answer closed-form questions over a tabular dataframe by writing and executing Python, almost always as a single rollout: one interpretation, one script, one result, accepted without comparison. The few pipelines that produce several candidates reconcile them by answer-agreement voting, which needs a single comparable scalar and always returns one, even when every candidate is wrong. We present GRACE (Generative, Ranked, Adaptive Code Ensemble), a production-oriented pipeline that instead runs parallel best-of-N selection specialised to dataframe code. N agents run concurrently under a strict, machine-checkable output contract, diversified by persona hints, per-retry rotation, and a two-regime temperature schedule. Rather than vote, GRACE judges the executed artefacts: a pairwise tournament pre-filters the pool and a domain-specialised guardrail either selects the best candidate or, when none is defensible, rejects the whole pool and abstains with a diagnosis (an option voting structurally lacks), which in turn drives an adaptive retry with per-agent feedback. On InfiAgent-DABench (DAEval, M = 246), using Qwen3.6-35B-A3B as the backbone, GRACE raises answer match from 39% for a single-rollout baseline to 71% (+32 pp, 1.8x), recovering 53% of previously wrong answers. On the same DAEval task, the smaller dense Qwen3.5-9B lifts answer match from 38% to 63% (+25 pp, 1.7x). A controlled 3 x 3 study that sweeps the number of parallel agents against the retry budget finds, on the 35B backbone, the two levers largely substitutable: once either one is generous, the other adds little, identifying a cheap operating point for deployment. The same directional pattern replicates on the independent DataBench benchmark, again on the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B backbone, where answer match rises by 15 percentage points (from 70% to 85%) across 400 sampled questions.

Multi-LLM Agentic AI at the Edge: Joint Agent Scheduling and Resource Allocation

Yuanyan Song, Kezhi Wang, Xinmian Xu, Yongmin Li
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-16 LLM & agentic AI

Multi-LLM agent scheduling with resource allocation and optimisation (AO, SCA, Gale-Shapley) at the edge; agentic and optimisation-based but not transport-focused.

Abstract

In this paper, an edge-enabled multi-large language model (LLM) agentic AI architecture is proposed, where one supervisor AI agent and multiple worker AI agents cooperatively execute users' requests at the edge. The supervisor AI agent decouples the user request into a series of sub-tasks and dynamically dispatches them to LLM-equipped worker AI agents via two agentic workflows, i.e., parallel and serial workflows, with a two-level retry mechanism to enhance execution reliability. To prolong the service duration of worker AI agents, the energy minimization problems are formulated by jointly considering AI agent selection and resource allocation, subject to task execution latency and quality of service (QoS) constraints. A resource allocation scheme based on alternating optimization (AO) is proposed. Specifically, for the parallel workflow, the scheme decouples the original problem into bandwidth allocation, retrieval selection and AI agent selection sub-problems, which are iteratively solved by the proposed successive convex approximation (SCA), Lagrangian relaxation (LR), and Gale-Shapley (GS) or LLM-based algorithms, respectively. For the serial workflow, the problem is similarly decoupled into service time allocation, retrieval selection and AI agent selection sub-problems; the optimal service time allocation is obtained via Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions, while the latter two sub-problems are addressed by extending the proposed algorithms to the serial scenario. Simulation results show that the proposed resource allocation scheme can reduce system energy consumption compared with other benchmarks, while experiments on a multi-LLM agentic AI system based on Qwen and Llama demonstrate improved task execution success rates.

Safely Mitigating Grid Imbalance: A Hybrid Distributional Wide-Margin Recourse Approach

Shoaib Hussain, Mostafa Farrokhabadi, Hamidreza Zareipour
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-17 Transport-grid integration

Safe RL for grid imbalance mitigation with soft open points in distribution networks; relevant to grid flexibility and RL for dispatch.

Abstract

This paper proposes the Multi-Agent Distributional Wide-Margin Policy Gradient (MADWMPG) framework: a dual-policy hybrid imitation and safe reinforcement learning (IL-SRL) method designed for safely mitigating grid imbalance in multiphase active distribution networks with distributed soft open points (SOPs). The framework comprises a wide-margin constraint barrier recourse (WCBR) module that seeks the maximal separation of constraint-compliant and unsafe actions and learns to proactively suppress exploratory violations. The framework addresses operational volatility using a novel adaptation of noncrossing quantile regression (NQR) distributional critics that track the complete distribution of policy returns. The set of behavior and recourse policies integrates an instantaneous physics-based and differentiable action projection mechanism that generates SOP constraint-compliant actions without relying on active learning. Policy safety is made responsive using an enhancement of conventional experience replay, and the safety performance of the framework is supported by probabilistic bounds derived in the paper. The proposed framework was trained and tested on a modified multiphase IEEE 123-bus test feeder. Results demonstrate its superiority to conventional reinforcement learning and SRL methods and show that it is highly competitive against the nonlinear AC optimal power flow.

Scientific Coding with Large Language Models: Evidence from Finite Element Analysis

Erfan Hamdi, Joel Shor, Emma Lejeune
arXiv arXiv cs.SE2026-11-16 LLM & agentic AI

Evaluates LLM agents writing and self-verifying scientific simulation code with execution feedback, relevant to agents running simulation pipelines and reliability.

Abstract

Large language models (LLMs) offer a potential route to automating scientific programming, but their usefulness depends on consistently producing numerically correct code. We extend FEM-Bench to 17 finite element tasks involving nonlinear and history-dependent mechanics and structural dynamics, and evaluate 15 models on implementation and test generation without execution feedback. The best model succeeds in 87.1% of single-pass implementation trials, but rankings differ between tracks. The leading implementer achieves 49.9% joint test success, while the leading tester reaches 82.6%. For six models, we compare single-pass generation with two agentic harnesses that allow code execution and revision. Agentic execution solves some tasks that never succeeded in single-pass trials and improves consistency on others, but gains vary across models and harnesses, and regressions also occur. Claude Opus 5 with SimpleAgent reaches 96.5% Pass@3, the probability of at least one success in three attempts, but 77.1% Pass$^3$, the probability that all three succeed. No model-harness combination exceeds 78.8% Pass$^3$. To understand why agents submit incorrect code after passing their own checks, we analyze 249 failed agentic trajectories and identify six categories of self-verification failure. The most common are inadequate test coverage (73.1%) and reliance on generic consistency checks (70.3%), and in 28.5% of cases the tests share an error with the implementation. Motivated by these findings, an exploratory configuration with separate coder and tester agents achieves the highest observed scores, with 97.6% Pass@3 and 88.8% Pass$^3$. These results identify consistency across attempts and independent verification as the main obstacles to trusting LLM-generated scientific code.

Social Welfare and Equilibrium in Distributed Microgrid Prosumer Coordination Under a Mixed Competitive-Cooperative Setting

Mahan Fakourifard, Elizabeth L. Ratnam, Mingxi Liu
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-16 Transport-grid integration

Distributed prosumer coordination with EV fleets on distribution networks, with convergence guarantees; directly relevant to transport-grid integration and price-based coordination.

Abstract

Price-based distributed prosumer coordination in distribution networks is fundamentally challenging when the aggregator pursues grid-level goals that couple prosumers' decisions, because the Lagrangian fails to separate and nodal prices are undefined under conventional dual decomposition. This paper develops a novel method that embeds mixed competitive-cooperative objectives exactly into prices, which are split into energy, congestion, and grid-objective components, closing the gap between the social welfare optimum and the competitive equilibrium. To compute the equilibrium, a distributed optimization algorithm is developed based on the projected Arrow-Hurwicz-Uzawa scheme. Explicit step-size rules are developed for the algorithm to guarantee $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{L})$ and $\mathcal{O}(1/L)$ ergodic convergence rates. Simulations on an IEEE 33-bus feeder that is connected to prosumers and electric vehicle fleets validate the accuracy and efficacy of the proposed methods.

Strengthening Compact Vehicle Routing Formulations through Arc-Flow Aggregation and Refinement

Julian Yarkony, Willem-Jan van Hoeve, Anthony Karahalios, Martin Karyamsetty et al.
arXiv arXiv math.OC2026-11-15 AI for optimisation

Arc-flow aggregation for VRPTW MILP formulations with cutting planes; strongly related to the researcher's MILP decomposition and routing interests.

Abstract

Arc-flow formulations can provide strong relaxations for mixed-integer program formulations of vehicle routing problems, but the associated networks may be prohibitively large. We study the setting in which multiple arc flow relaxations may be applied, and introduce a dual-guided aggregation and refinement framework that constructs substantially smaller arc-flow formulations while recovering the LP bound of the original networks. Dual information is used both to identify states that can be aggregated without weakening the bound and to guide node disaggregation. We show that the resulting procedure converges finitely to the arc-flow bound of the full arc-flow formulation. The framework retains the original decision variables, allowing cutting planes to be added directly in the original variable space. Moreover, it produces an explicit MILP formulation without relying on column generation or pricing to determine the arc-flow relaxation bound. We apply our approach to the vehicle routing problem with time windows for which we define two arc flow relaxations, as well as two families of cutting planes over the original variable space. Computational experiments show that strong bounds can be obtained using substantially aggregated networks. The resulting root bounds are competitive with those obtained by the branch-and-price solver RouteOpt, while retaining a formulation that can be solved directly by a general-purpose MILP solver.

ToolGraph as a Q-Function for Test-Time Scaling of Tool-Use Agents

Ke Shen, Ankita Bhaumik, Li Dong, Shengjie Liu et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-16 LLM & agentic AI

Graph-based value function for tool-use agent chaining calls; agentic planning with multi-step value, useful for agent design.

Abstract

Tool-use agents must chain interdependent calls, yet which outputs satisfy which later inputs in a deployed catalog is not something a general-purpose language model reliably infers from tool descriptions alone. We compile that structure once into a reusable external representation. ToolGraph is a directed, weighted dependency graph over a tool catalog: parameter matching and interventional probes, which execute a candidate chain with and without its proposed predecessor, propose edges whose evidence is combined into an edge confidence. Prior work uses such graphs to retrieve tools, search for tool paths, or score the next call. We ask instead whether the same structure can supply a \emph{multi-step} value by composing graph paths into a goal-conditioned action value \(Q(s,a,g)\). We study three estimators (dynamic programming over entity sets, a GraphSAGE estimator distilled from it, and an LLM scorer conditioned on graph evidence) plus a verbalizer that returns action--goal feedback to the agent, which keeps final action selection. Across two tool-use benchmarks and three backbones, we compare value ranking against personalized-PageRank retrieval over the identical graph, and against direct and reflective agents. Composition pays off only when the estimator also reads the concrete state: the LLM estimator improves on PageRank in every benchmark--backbone setting, by \(8.3\) points on average, while the two purely structural estimators fall below it, because a value defined over tool types cannot separate a correct tool called with a wrong argument. Graph construction is a one-time cost; we report it separately rather than assume it amortized away (at our scale it is comparable to request-time cost) and sweep four knobs controlling how much graph evidence to expose against success and tokens.

TrainExplorer: Autonomous LLM Post-Training via Plan-and-Decouple Exploration

Xuannan Liu, Tian Lan, Zekun Li, Xing Cui et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-11-16 LLM & agentic AI

Multi-agent LLM framework that plans and executes autonomous post-training experiments with decoupled attribution, akin to agents running optimisation pipelines.

Abstract

LLM-based agents can automate model post-training, accelerating progress toward recursive self-improvement. Despite their promise, existing approaches remain under-explored and face two main challenges: (1) strategy lock-in that limits exploration beyond the initial strategy; and (2) coupled execution that obscures the effects of individual modifications. To address these challanges, we propose TrainExplorer, a multi-agent framework for autonomous post-training, that incorporates strategy planning and execution decoupling, to achieve broaden strategy exploration. VTT: Reading Vehicle Size and Powertrain Type from Motion Trajectory Across Unseen Car Models

Yuhang Wang, Yuechen Guo, Jingxin Yang, Chuheng Wei et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-11-17 E-bus scheduling & charging

Infers vehicle size and powertrain from CAN and trajectory data; useful for electrified fleet data and simulation inputs.

Abstract

Vehicle size and powertrain information can enrich trajectory data for traffic analysis and simulation. We present VTT, a dataset and benchmark for identifying these attributes from motion and controller area network (CAN) signals across unseen car models. The dataset contains 9,062 drives, about 3,950 h, from 1,431 vehicles and 174 car models. It provides three footprint-based size classes, powertrain labels, and evaluation on unseen car models and unseen drivers. Using 60-s windows, we compare four sequence architectures, statistical features and physical features proposed by large language models. Training with auxiliary physical targets, data augmentation and task-specific weighting raises drive-level macro-F1 to 92.6-93.0% for battery-electric versus combustion vehicles and from 62.9% to 67.1% for size classification. On public logs from 4,061 additional drivers, the full-input model identifies powertrain correctly for 86 of 88 car models; size accuracy is 63.2%, close to the internal vehicle-level result of 63.5%. Physical-feature analysis links powertrain recognition to deceleration behaviour and size recognition to steering response. Errors near size-class boundaries and limited gains from longer observations highlight the importance of label resolution and training-fleet coverage for cross-model generalisation.

Value and Policy Calibration from Execution Feedback for Socially Compatible Driving

Sen Shen, Ning Liu, Xiangrui Kong, Yingwei Li et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.RO2026-11-15 LLM & agentic AI

World-model RL for AV intersection behaviour affecting human-driven vehicles; adjacent to operations decisions but not agentic LLM or transit-specific.

Abstract

Autonomous vehicle (AV) decisions at intersections affect the delay, braking, and motion smoothness of surrounding human-driven vehicles (HDVs). Learning socially compatible behavior requires connecting imagined interaction outcomes with feedback from executed actions. We propose a world-model reinforcement learning framework combines imagined policy improvement with direct value and policy calibration from finalized interactions, balancing AV progress with HDV interaction burden. An interaction-aware latent model supports imagination, while off-policy correction and replay updates enable observed rewards and transitions to supervise both the critic and actor. Tests under out-of-distribution traffic shifts further show lower ego jerk and HDV acceleration variation than PIWM at comparable passage times.

Weight-space programming for tractor guidance controller: large language models natively write neural network weights that steer a real tractor

Timo Oksanen
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-11-16 LLM & agentic AI

LLMs directly write neural controller weights to steer a real tractor with simulation feedback, relevant to LLM-driven control design and vehicle guidance.

Abstract

Weight-space programming is a process where a large language model reads and writes the weights of a neural network directly, guided by a natural language specification, without an optimiser or gradient descent. This study applies it to automatic tractor guidance, where a 253-parameter neural network steers along paths of lines and arcs and must be adapted when the machine changes, such as a slower steering actuator. GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 (also 5.2) edited the network for our research tractor using a small budget of simulated evaluations to repair a network trained for another tractor and write a controller into a network with zero weights. We compared them with an evolution strategy, gradient descent and pure pursuit in simulation and on the real tractor. Both models read the network and analysed the closed loop with control theory correctly, and Fable's repair without simulation succeeded only occasionally. With simulated feedback, both models repaired the trained network in single sessions and wrote a working controller from zero weights, whereas from zero the evolution strategy found none. The controllers written from zero were sparse and in a classical cascaded structure; one model edited the trained network while the other replaced it. Gradient descent reached higher accuracy with far more simulation, and all language model controllers steered the real tractor without intervention, with errors of the same order as pure pursuit. Language models can program the weights of a neural controller directly, much as control engineers design one. We call this Weight-Space Engineering.
