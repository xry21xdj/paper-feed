Analyzing Simultaneous Charging Factors of Electric Vehicles Using Monte Carlo Simulation: A Case Study in Phu Quoc City, Vietnam

Tien Khai Nguyen, Trong Nghia Le
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-04 Transport-grid integration

Monte Carlo estimate of simultaneous EV charging including electric buses, informing transformer sizing and time-of-use planning; adjacent to bus depot grid impact.

Abstract

This study estimates the simultaneous charging factor (SCF) of electric vehicles (EVs) in Phu Quoc City, Vietnam, using a Monte Carlo simulation. Three planning scenarios (2030, 2035, and 2040) are developed from population-based EV uptake assumptions and vehicle-specific charging probability profiles over 24 hours, reflecting distinct operating patterns of motorbikes, cars, taxis, and buses. For each vehicle class and scenario, 1,000 simulation runs are performed to compute SCF and its uncertainty, reported via 95% confidence intervals. Results indicate that SCF decreases as EV penetration increases, implying more temporally dispersed charging demand and a reduced likelihood of extreme coincidence. For electric buses, SCF declines from 0.76 (2030) to 0.59 (2040), and other vehicle groups show similarly decreasing trends across the horizon. These SCF estimates provide practical inputs for grid-aware charging infrastructure planning, including diversity factors for sizing distribution transformers and feeders, identifying peak-hour stress, and informing demand management measures such as time of use tariffs and basic smart charging controls.

InterSim: Innovative Intersection Digital Twins for Human-in-the-Loop Driving Simulation

Luyang Jiang, Chuheng Wei, Yaguang Li, Shaoqi Wang et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.CE2026-11-04 Traffic simulation & calibration

Unity-based human-in-the-loop intersection digital twin with surrogate safety and eco-driving studies; relevant simulation methodology but not calibrated bus operations.

Abstract

Innovative intersection designs can improve roadway safety and operational efficiency, but their uncommon geometries may create challenges for driver comprehension during early deployment. Evaluating these designs through full-scale field implementation can also be costly. This paper presents InterSim, a Unity game engine-based human-in-the-loop (HITL) driving simulator that constructs digital twins of site-specific innovative intersections in a virtual environment. InterSim links the real world and the simulation world through two closed cycles: a digital twin cycle that builds each intersection twin from agency design and traffic data and returns evidence from simulation to the agency, and an HITL cycle in which human drivers or automated driving algorithms operate the ego vehicle under identical scenario conditions. The research capabilities of InterSim are demonstrated through two case studies: a surrogate safety study compares post-encroachment time distributions between a Reduced Conflict Intersection and a standard intersection under different traffic volumes; an eco-driving study compares human drivers with a rule-based agent using vehicle-to-infrastructure information and reveals differences in emission and driving behavior. These studies demonstrate that InterSim provides a reusable digital twin platform for investigating innovative intersection designs from diverse perspectives. Demo video and open-source code can be accessed at https://purduedigitaltwin.github.io/InterSim/
