Electric Vehicle Scheduling and Carbon-Aware Energy Dispatch for Charging Stations

Fulong Yao, Yiming Xu, Maurizio Albano, Liana Cipcigan et al.
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-10-27 E-bus scheduling & charging

Greedy vehicle scheduling and TD3 battery dispatch for charging stations with PV and carbon-aware costs, directly relevant to charger scheduling and energy cost.

Abstract

Electric vehicle (EV) charging stations integrating photovoltaic (PV) generation, battery storage, and bidirectional grid exchange must coordinate access to limited chargers with energy supply while considering both energy cost and carbon emissions. This paper proposes a solver-free greedy heuristic (SFGH) for dynamic vehicle scheduling (VS) and a twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient (TD3) controller for carbon-aware energy dispatch (ED). SFGH assigns EVs according to reductions in service delays, and TD3 controls battery operation to balance energy costs and carbon emissions while meeting the resulting charging demand. The model allows both PV and battery exports, accounting for export revenue and carbon credits. Simulations on year-long case studies show that SFGH--TD3 achieves the lowest accumulated service delay, energy cost, and carbon emissions among the evaluated heuristic and multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (DRL) configurations.

Cooperative EV Charging Assignment Using Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning: A Comparison of QMIX and Independent Q-Learning Under Urban Traffic Uncertainty

Nour-Eddine Moumni, Rachid Alaoui, Driss Kiouach
arXiv arXiv cs.MA2026-10-27 E-bus scheduling & charging

QMIX versus IQL multi-agent RL for EV charging assignment across stations in SUMO, a clear method and application match.

Abstract

The allocation of electric vehicles to public charging stations is increasingly constrained by the fact that several heterogeneous facilities often serve the same stream of requests while observing only partial local information. An accept/reject decision that is defensible for one station may nevertheless reduce the efficiency of the charging district as a whole. We formulate this operational problem as a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP) and examine whether Centralized Training with Decentralized Execution (CTDE), instantiated through QMIX, improves assignment performance relative to a cooperative Independent Q-Learning baseline (IQL). The evaluation uses a six-station urban network simulated with SUMO, five independent random seeds, 200 training episodes, and four traffic flux scenarios ranging from 100 to 250 vehicles/hour. Under this protocol, QMIX reaches a mean reward per evaluated decision of +0.776, versus +0.578 for IQL (delta = +0.198, seed-paired Cohens dz = 2.98, exact two-sided Wilcoxon p = 0.0625, the minimum attainable value at this sample size), and raises the acceptance rate from 72.4% to 94.0% (delta = +21.6 pp, dz = 5.24, Wilcoxon p = 0.0625). A five-variant ablation suggests this gain is primarily attributable to the CTDE architecture when holding cooperative reward terms fixed, though not formally isolated with a single seed. By contrast, cooperative reward shaping and experience sharing show no clear descriptive benefit and may slightly reduce performance. These findings support the view that, for multi-station charging assignment, coordination should be embedded in the learning architecture rather than added only through heuristic cooperation terms.

Dual-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Vehicle Scheduling and Multi-Energy Dispatch in Electric-Hydrogen-Transport Systems

Fulong Yao, Yiming Xu, Maurizio Albano, Liana Cipcigan et al.
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-10-29 Transport-grid integration

Couples electric bus-like vehicle scheduling with multi-energy dispatch using RL, directly touching the researcher's scheduling and energy-coupling interests.

Abstract

Electric-hydrogen-transport systems (EHTSs) require coordinated scheduling of electric and hydrogen vehicles together with multi-energy dispatch. These operations form a sequential decision process: upstream vehicle scheduling (VS) determines service allocation and order, thereby endogenously shaping the electricity and hydrogen demands faced by downstream energy dispatch (ED). This paper develops a dual-agent reinforcement learning framework tailored to the heterogeneous decision structures. The vehicle-scheduling agent combines proximal policy optimisation (PPO) with a Plackett-Luce policy to learn vehicle-priority permutations, which are then mapped to feasible charger and refueller assignments through a greedy allocation. The energy-dispatch agent employs twin delayed deep deterministic policy gradient (TD3) for continuous storage and energy-flow control. The directional coupling between the two decision layers is analysed, revealing that updates to the upstream scheduling policy can shift the demand distribution experienced by the downstream dispatcher. A three-stage training mechanism is therefore introduced, combining layer-wise pretraining with concurrent fine-tuning to mitigate the effects of scheduling-induced distribution shifts on downstream ED learning. Year-long case studies show that the proposed framework achieves the lowest vehicle-service delay and energy cost among the 11 evaluated methods, demonstrating effective coordination between vehicle scheduling and multi-energy dispatch.

Algebraic World Model: From Exploration to Proofs

Guancheng Pan, Qixin Tan, Yang Yuan, Andrew Chi-Chih Yao
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-10-29 LLM agents for transport/OR

LLM-driven world-model construction with Lean proofs for reachability; a generic agent paper with limited transport or OR overlap despite its agentic framing.

Abstract

AI has made remarkable progress in mathematics, solving challenging problems with machine-checkable proofs in Lean, yet typically relying on predefined mathematical definitions and formal specifications. This raises a natural question: can it reason rigorously about an unknown world? We introduce the Algebraic World Model (AWM), a framework that turns interaction with unfamiliar environments into explicit mathematical models supporting formal reasoning. AWM explores the environment and develops executable Python hypotheses, continually testing and revising them against observations. It then structures these hypotheses into typed objects and shared rules, simplifies their structure through category-theory-guided transformations, and formalizes the resulting model in Lean 4 to prove reachability and impossibility. AWM can formally prove that a goal is unreachable, even beyond explored trajectories. On two impossible game levels, it constructs closed, kernel-checked Lean proofs excluding every legal winning sequence in all six runs, while Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, equipped with coding tools, produce none in twelve baseline runs. Four of AWM's six initial bounds are correct, compared with one for Opus 5.5 and none for GPT-6 Astra; given only feedback that a cheaper win exists, AWM revises its rules and constructs new proofs, reaching six of six correct bounds. Moreover, AWM produces interpretable mathematical models whose rules can be inspected and tested step by step. Across all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games, these models enable Lean to replay the winning trajectories of all 183 levels and generalize beyond observed play, predicting unseen transitions and detecting changes in game mechanics. With Claude Opus 5, AWM clears all levels with a score of 99.9, while its certified winning trajectories use only 43% of the human baseline's actions.

Distribution Grid Reconfiguration and Restoration via Linearizable Multi-phase Power Flow Modeling

Young-ho Cho, Hao Zhu
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-10-26 Transport-grid integration

MILP-compatible neural power-flow model for distribution network reconfiguration and restoration; relevant to grid integration and MILP formulations.

Abstract

Accurate and tractable power-flow (PF) modeling is essential for distribution grid topology optimization, where the presence of discrete branch-status decisions therein significantly increase the computational complexity. To tackle this challenge, we develop a topology-adaptive, low-complexity neural piecewise-linear (N-PWL) PF model. We propose a sequential layer design that can directly incorporate branch status variables into the PF mapping from branch flows to nodal injections. As a result, our N-PWL model not only preserves the PF branch-node consistency but also facilitates the representation of any unseen topologies without retraining. A low-complexity model design is further pursued to attain a linear NN parameter scaling by using a fixed-size area partition of the feeder. The resulting scalable N-PWL model enables mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) formulations of feeder topology optimization tasks, including distribution network reconfiguration (DNR) and restoration ordering problems (ROP). Numerical studies on the unbalanced, multi-phase IEEE 123-bus feeder demonstrate our N-PWL model outperforms existing end-to-end baselines in PF prediction and, more importantly, can fill the gap by achieving accurate representation of any unseen topologies. For solving DNR/ROP, our model can significantly improve the AC feasibility over linearized PF models, with a notable reduction of computation time compared with AC-PF based nonlinear formulations, particularly under a large number of topology decisions.

GNN-Guided Network Reduction for Centralized Urban Traffic Routing

Nikolai Antonov, Lukáš Chrpa, Andrii Nyporko, Jan Hůla
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-10-27 Traffic simulation & calibration

GNN-guided network reduction for MILP-based urban traffic routing; traffic-optimisation methods that the researcher may find useful.

Abstract

We consider the centralized traffic routing (CTR) problem, where a central planner assigns routes to origin-destination (OD) vehicle flows to minimize congestion-dependent segment cost. Prior work formulates CTR efficiently as a mixed-integer linear program (MILP), but scaling this formulation to the full road network is computationally prohibitive within an operational time budget. Bounded Routes and Smart Routes speed up optimization using precomputed reduced networks; however, these methods do not account for vehicle information within each OD flow. Motivated by this gap, we propose a learning-based urban network reduction approach. A dedicated graph neural network (GNN) first scores road segments for each OD flow using the road network and vehicle-flow information. Highly ranked segments are then incorporated into complete OD routes, and the original segment-flow MILP is solved on the resulting reduced road network. The GNN thus guides the reduction of the road network, while the solver enforces feasibility and determines the routing. Experiments on four real urban networks -- Prague-Dejvice, London, New York, and Sydney -- show that the proposed approach achieves the smallest relative objective difference to the $30$-second full-graph reference in every city under both traffic regimes. Averaged across cities, this difference is more than six times lower than that of the best competing method. Moreover, traffic simulation results show that the optimization gains of the proposed method lead to more efficient routing, with the highest aggregate arrival rate and a competitive trip duration per arrival percentage point.

LLM-Assisted Optimization for Simulation-Based Redesign

Chan-Eui Song, Jeremy J. Michalek, Christopher McComb
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-10-29 LLM agents for transport/OR

Integrates LLM guidance with black-box solvers for simulation-based redesign on an EV battery case; relevant LLM-plus-optimisation method.

Abstract

Engineering design frequently involves redesigning a prior solution to adapt to changes in context, such as material prices or system requirements. Redesign often leverages simulators too complex and/or computationally intensive for traditional numerical solvers, which lack engineering knowledge and context, to ensure global solution quality. Large language models (LLMs) can interpret semantic information, including engineering knowledge about the system and changes in context, to help guide search, and recent studies have shown that incorporating this knowledge into optimization can improve search performance. However, how best to combine LLMs with numerical solvers in redesign remains unclear. We develop and test an integrated LLM-optimization approach for redesign and compare three methods for leveraging LLMs (warm start, variable fixing and ReAct) and four black-box solvers (Bayesian optimization, genetic algorithms, mesh adaptive direct search and random search) on an electric vehicle battery pack cost minimization case study across four types of redesign scenarios. We find that the integrated approach can match feasible design discovery of the numerical-solver baselines with 80% fewer simulator evaluations while tripling the fraction of runs that find near-optimal solutions. We find that the best LLM-solver integration approach and the way the prior solution is leveraged depend on the structure of the design requirement change, and that the benefit of increasing LLM model size grows with the extent to which the LLM controls the search. These results show clear potential for LLMs in redesign tasks for guiding or replacing numerical solvers.
