MAC+: Mobility-Aware Co-Expansion Planning for Distribution Grid and Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure

Yi Ju, Lunlong Li, Jingchun Wang, Scott Moura
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-09 Transport-grid integration

Co-expansion planning of distribution grids and EV charging with itinerary-flexible charging and optimisation solved by iterated price response; closely matches grid integration interests.

Abstract

We study the least-cost co-expansion of power distribution networks and public charging ports to accommodate the growth of electric vehicles (EVs). Our approach, MAC+, exploits the flexibility of charging along each EV's itinerary and coordinates it across the fleet to reduce the peak violations of each feeder's hosting capacity and port capacity. This coordination is an extremely large, spatio-temporally coupled optimization problem with binary plug-in variables. We solve it by iterated price response (IPR), which repeatedly posts a two-tier tariff on feeder load and port occupancy, lets millions of EVs best-respond in parallel, and certifies the optimality gap at every iteration. We develop a custom exact dynamic-programming algorithm that solves each EV's best-response problem, a mixed-integer quadratic program, about 20 times faster than off-the-shelf solvers. The case study covers the San Francisco Bay Area at 30% EV adoption, with 1.8 million EVs on week-long itineraries and 1,303 feeder service regions, each with a utility-published hosting capacity and a port capacity set by its existing public ports. On this instance, IPR reaches a certified gap below 10% in a few hours. MAC+ saves about $15 billion of the upgrade investment that unmanaged charging would require. It also needs over 90% less investment than charging that is fully coordinated but flexible only within each session, and over 80% less than charging that is flexible along the itinerary but uncoordinated.

Prospective Action-Resource Graph Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning for Electric HGV Fleets under Shared Charging Infrastructure

Yiming Xu, Fulong Yao, Maurizio Albano, Rigel Gjomemo et al.
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-09 Transport-grid integration

MARL with graph coordination for electric HGV fleets sharing charging infrastructure; directly on electrified fleet charging scheduling the researcher works on.

Abstract

Electric heavy goods vehicle (eHGV) fleet coordination requires each vehicle to choose driving, charging and resting actions whose consequences depend on shared infrastructure. Existing multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) methods mainly represent current states, leaving action-specific futures and resource interactions to be learned indirectly. We develop Horizon-Preview and Graph-Coordinated Proximal Policy Optimisation (HGC-PPO), which integrates horizon preview and candidate-resource graph coordination into the PPO decision architecture. Horizon preview represents each legal action through its short-horizon operational consequences. Graph message passing then updates these candidates through station, charger-type and time-slot resources shared across the fleet. On the 794km Folkestone-Glasgow task, HGC-PPO achieved the lowest mean charging cost among five RL methods at £504.32 per vehicle, with a fleet completion time of 24.920h. It also completed the task 1.763h faster and cost £36.51 less per vehicle than a replay-verified finite-budget MILP benchmark. Across six unseen routes and fleet sizes from 5 to 20 vehicles, HGC-PPO delivered the lowest macro-average completion time, charging cost and charging frequency among the RL methods. A 2 by 2 ablation showed that the full preview-graph architecture performed best across all four operational metrics. These results show that prospective action information and fleet-wide resource coordination improve eHGV decisions under charging and driver-hours constraints.

JoMoHa: Joint Model--Harness Optimization with Reinforcement Learning

Xudong Wu, Guangchen Lan, Mingqi Yuan, Jiayu Chen
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-11-08 LLM & agentic AI

Jointly trains model weights and executable harness via RL for language agents; agent-design and self-improvement relevant to agentic AI interests.

Abstract

The performance of language agents depends jointly on model weights and the harness. Optimizing either alone can be constrained by the other component, limiting overall performance gains. Our analysis of naive alternating training reveals progressive incompatibility between the model and harness, which increasingly interferes with task execution and may also undermine model training, leading to performance degradation. To coordinate the evolution of models and harnesses, we introduce JoMoHa, to our knowledge the first framework to jointly train an executable harness generation policy and a task execution policy through reinforcement learning. JoMoHa outperforms the evaluated weight-only and harness-only baselines and achieves the highest success rates among the evaluated methods on ALFWorld, WebShop, and DBBench, averaging 70.3% and reaching 97.0% on ALFWorld. By learning both task execution and runtime improvement from its own generated trajectories, JoMoHa performs recursive self-improvement (RSI).

PARIS: A multi-agent LLM framework for end-to-end, PRISMA-guided systematic literature reviews with pre-synthesis corpus quality assessment

Christophe Cruz, Hussam Ghanem, Samir Jabbar, Ait Kbir et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-09 LLM & agentic AI

Multi-agent LLM framework orchestrating 15 agents through a full research workflow with validation checkpoints; agentic and operations-style, though not transport-specific.

Abstract

Systematic literature reviews (SRs) remain slow and labor-intensive, and most automation tools address single stages of the workflow. We present PARIS (PRISMA Agentic Research Intelligence System), an open-source framework in which a conductor agent running in Claude Code orchestrates 15 specialized large language model (LLM) agents, from scoping a research question to exporting a manuscript draft. Agents are specified declaratively, coordinate exclusively through persisted artifacts that the conductor validates before every transition, and delegate retrieval, bibliometric analysis and scoring to deterministic Python utilities. Three human checkpoints gate study inclusion, corpus adequacy and the synthesis outline. We also introduce QualityScore, a pre-synthesis index that aggregates normalized network, bibliometric, semantic and process sub-scores into a 0-100 grade. Two case studies illustrate both: AI chatbots and university students' learning (388 records, 100 included) and AI-assisted SR methods (131 records, 123 included, QualityScore 69.1 recomputed with a proxy network sub-score). They show end-to-end feasibility across domains and the diagnostic use of QualityScore: in the first run, corpus improvements chosen at the corpus-adequacy checkpoint raised the score from 74.6 to 81.9 and the number of pre-2023 studies from 1 to 19. Audits of both runs exposed failure modes: incomplete retrieval, counts that drifted between agents, unrecorded or delegated checkpoint decisions and a grade that depends on how citation data are handled. PARIS fully supports 8 of the 27 PRISMA 2020 items and partially supports 11. Output quality has so far been judged only by an internal reviewer agent; independent validation against human reviews is the next step.

A Physical Testbed for Self-Driving Laboratories: Comparing Bayesian and Agentic Optimization in a Minimal Closed-Loop Color Formulation Task

Tony Qin, Ziheng Wu, Maximilian Schommer, Roya Amini-Naieni et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.RO2026-11-08 AI for optimisation

Physical self-driving lab testbed comparing Bayesian optimisation with an LLM agentic optimizer in closed loop; relevant to LLM-driven optimisation.

Abstract

Self-driving laboratories (SDLs) integrate laboratory robotics and automation with AI-driven decision-making to close the design-build-test-learn (DBTL) cycle. However, physical testbeds that combine off-the-shelf hardware and AI into a functioning closed-loop workflow remain in early stages of development, and their integration with commercial laboratory automation is comparatively less developed. In this work, we develop a physical testbed that assembles and integrates commercially available laboratory automation instruments and demonstrate continuous, closed-loop, autonomous operation over 24 cumulative hours of unattended experimentation. We validate this platform on a minimal closed-loop discovery task in which the system autonomously formulates aqueous dye mixtures to match a target absorbance spectrum. The system completed six independent campaigns, each consisting of 48 closed-loop iterations over approximately four hours. In every campaign, the system autonomously selected, dispensed, and measured each mixture, with an AI-driven decision-making algorithm determining subsequent formulations without human intervention. Using this testbed, we compared the performance of two AI-driven decision-making algorithms, traditional Bayesian optimization (BO) and an agentic approach, in selecting mixtures under a fixed total-volume constraint. The agentic optimizer matched target absorbance spectra using fewer iterations than BO, with reductions up to 52%, and converged on a solution for one target that BO failed to match within 48 iterations. These results demonstrate a reference physical platform for SDLs that can evaluate both established and emerging AI strategies, including large language model (LLM)-directed optimization, before their deployment in real-world applications where experiments may be costly and time-consuming to prepare.

APO2CS: Advantage-Aware Preference Optimization for Two-Level Constructive Search in Combinatorial Optimization

Kaixi Yang, Linbo Qiao, Wei Shang, Yanqi Shi et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-11-09 AI for optimisation

Preference optimisation for neural constructive combinatorial solvers with advantage-aware objectives; relevant learning-to-optimise method, though not on scheduling with EVs.

Abstract

Preference optimization has emerged as a promising alternative to reward-based training for neural combinatorial optimization. However, existing methods typically rely on binary pairwise labels, which discard the magnitude of solution-quality differences and provide supervision only at the complete-solution level. We propose Advantage-aware Preference Optimization for Two-level Constructive Search (APO2CS), a preference-based training objective for constructive neural solvers that integrates both macro-level and micro-level supervision. At the macro level, APO2CS enriches pairwise preferences with an advantage term derived from objective-value differences. At the micro level, it compares partial trajectories, thereby providing denser supervision throughout the sequential construction process. The resulting objective can be used either to train a policy from scratch or to fine-tune a pretrained one. Experimental results show that APO2CS improves the expressiveness and search capability of preference learning for combinatorial optimization, while demonstrating strong effectiveness and adaptability across multiple combinatorial optimization tasks.

AutoBuddy: Evaluating Proactive, Always-On Agents on Task Discovery and Execution in a Simulated Workplace

Shiqi Chen, Daniel Madrigal Diaz, Wei-Ning Chen, Gbola Afonja et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-09 LLM & agentic AI

Evaluates proactive always-on LLM agents completing tasks in simulated workplace; agentic execution relevant to operational decision making.

Abstract

As LLM agents become more autonomous, they are increasingly expected to run as always-on assistants on a user's behalf, identifying work to be done and completing it without being asked. Yet existing benchmarks usually give the agent its task, whereas in knowledge work the requests sit in the user's workspace and the agent must first recognise which obligations exist. We study proactive task discovery and execution, in which an agent must infer a user's pending tasks from their workspace and complete them in the systems where the work lives. This setting poses two challenges. For the agent, the tasks are buried in a large volume of mail, chat, documents and records. For evaluation, a benchmark needs a simulated organisation whose people, events and documents are mutually consistent, so that tasks arise realistically and their correct outcomes are known. We introduce AutoBuddy-Bench, a simulator that addresses these challenges. It models an organisation's employees, projects, business systems and event history as a temporal graph, renders it into realistic workspace artefacts, derives ground-truth tasks from the events that create obligations, and computes each task's outcome from the world state; execution is judged by the agent's changes to the workspace. To evaluate current LLMs, we build AutoBuddy, a reference agent with interchangeable backbone LLMs: it identifies tasks from the workspace, grounding each in its source records, and executes each in a separate session with write access. Varying only the backbone, we find a systematic shift in where tasks are lost: weaker models fail mainly at discovery, intermediate models identify tasks but often stop short of carrying them out, and the strongest models fail mainly during execution, most often on business-system operations. Discovery precision is only weakly related to execution quality, suggesting the two are distinct capabilities.

Decision Aware Origin-Destination Demand Estimation with Structurally Constrained Data

Xianzhe Wang, Yueyue Fan, Johannes Royset
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-11-09 Traffic simulation & calibration

Decision-aware origin-destination demand estimation under network constraints; transport demand modelling adjacent to simulation calibration work.

Abstract

Origin-destination (OD) demand plays an important role in transportation system design and management applications. Conventional OD estimation methods typically judge estimation quality based on how estimated demand aligns with observed traffic data and historical demand patterns. We argue that data alignment alone is insufficient; how errors in OD estimates influence downstream decision quality should also be considered. This paper develops a decision aware OD demand estimation framework that leverages structurally constrained traffic data to characterize an admissible demand region, within which we identify OD estimates that would lead to least regret in the downstream decision. Therefore, our OD estimates are both data-consistent and decision-relevant. Methodologically, we approximate the admissible demand region induced by physical network constraints through a geometric representation, which regularizes the search over feasible OD estimates. Within this regularized region, we use a convex surrogate of downstream regret to obtain a tractable decision aware optimization problem. Numerical experiments show that the proposed framework consistently reduces decision regret relative to conventional estimation benchmarks, demonstrating the value of aligning OD demand estimation with downstream decision quality.

Embodied First Exam

Kangrui Wang, Qineng Wang, Changhe Chen, Pingyue Zhang et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.RO2026-11-09 LLM & agentic AI

Benchmark for coding agents controlling robots via code, policy training or direct control; agents that plan and execute, relevant to agentic evaluation.

Abstract

Early successes on agentic robotics point to a potential paradigm shift toward using coding agents to control robots. Existing robotics benchmarks are built around visuomotor policy learning, leaving it open whether coding agents can reliably control diverse robots under physical-world constraints. We therefore introduce the Embodied First Exam, a unified, comprehensive, and agent-native evaluation framework where coding agents can choose their own control approach: direct control, code to control, or train a policy to control. We hope it serves as a first exam for coding agents before they enter the physical world, with 1,156 tasks in 35 suites on 47 robots, including 9 new suites that go beyond pick-and-place to spatial puzzles, dexterous manipulation, multi-robot interaction, dynamic whole-body control, and sim-to-sim engineering. Each task can be run in up to three evaluation settings. The Standard Setting mimics the real world: the agent receives only sensor observations and, by default, cannot reset the environment. Its Realtime variant keeps the environment running while the agent reasons. The Privileged Setting gives the agent access to the full simulator state and unlimited resets, allowing it to search for an oracle solution for training purposes. Success rates in this setting serve as an approximate upper bound. On the 456 core tasks, the strongest of eight agents, GPT-6 Astra in Codex, achieves a success rate of 34% in the Standard Setting, leaving much room for improvement. To see where agents fail, we label the stage at which failed attempts go wrong and compare failures across agents, settings, and stage categories.

Evaluating AI Agents for Industrial-Level Chip Floorplanning: A Framework and Empirical Study

Nova-Silicon Team
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-09 AI for optimisation

Evaluates AI agents on chip floorplanning optimisation with physical quality metrics; agentic optimisation in an engineering layout setting.

Abstract

Floorplanning is an early step in turning a logical circuit into a physical chip: it positions large circuit modules and constrains the space available for subsequent logic placement and wiring. These decisions affect congestion and timing, but their physical consequences emerge through downstream implementation. For AI agents, the task requires translating engineering constraints into spatial decisions whose quality cannot be judged by successful tool execution alone. We present an evaluation framework that connects submitted layouts to a prescribed downstream implementation flow, checks geometric validity, measures physical quality, and reports resource use. On one industrial design whose 99 macros occupy approximately 71\% of a fixed L-shaped layout region, we compare our specialized system Nova-SI with general-purpose engineering agents and agents restricted to file access, obtaining complete physical measurements for 39 layout outputs. Under our 0--100 composite quality score, Nova-SI achieves a mean of 57.97 without online feedback from the prescribed power-grid and placement evaluation, compared with 31.17 for the strongest measured general-purpose configuration, GPT-6 Astra. The difference is supported by raw congestion and timing measurements and persists across scoring-sensitivity checks. Layout-level analysis further shows that similar global utilization can conceal substantially different local congestion. Together, the protocol and case study assess agents through the physical consequences of their delivered layouts and provide evidence about system performance on this industrial instance.

Feasibility-Aware Learning for Network-Constrained Unit Commitment

Hien Thanh Doan, Yeji Lim, Hongseok Kim
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-08 AI for optimisation

Learning-based network-constrained unit commitment without MIP at inference; an ML-for-optimisation method close to learned scheduling approaches.

Abstract

Learning-based approaches to network-constrained unit commitment (NCUC) usually still rely on an optimization solver to repair or complete their predictions, which limits the speedup they can deliver. This paper proposes FALCON-UC, a feasibility-aware graph learning framework that predicts commitment and dispatch jointly and produces final schedules without solving a mixed-integer program at inference time. It combines an LSTM--GraphSAGE predictor over a fully connected generator graph, a differentiable correction that embeds commitment-dependent dispatch bounds, power balance, and PTDF-based branch flows in training, and deterministic commitment, dispatch, and ramp-coupled reserve repairs at inference. On the 2383-bus system, FALCON-UC achieves $99.09\%$ commitment accuracy and a $0.4350\%$ mean absolute cost gap with all evaluated constraints within tolerance at $0.0562$ s per instance, a $2.27\times10^{3}$ speedup over the solver. MTLR R-SCUC-FL is slightly more accurate but still needs an optimization solver to complete each schedule, which takes $262.2$ s per instance, $4.67\times10^{3}$ times the FALCON-UC runtime. A label-only baseline without repair violates the balance and branch limits on every 2383-bus test instance, and FALCON-UC also meets all evaluated constraint tolerances on a 1354-bus system.

Graph-Based RL for Sequential Rendezvous Planning with Multiple Patrolling USVs

Seung Woo Han, Hyeon Bin Yeo, Seung Heon Oh
arXiv arXiv cs.RO2026-11-09 AI for optimisation

Graph neural network RL for sequential rendezvous scheduling of patrol USVs with tardiness objectives; a scheduling-RL method relevant as background, though maritime rather than bus fleets.

Abstract

In patrol missions involving multiple unmanned surface vehicles (USVs), a designated command USV can support fleet management by sequentially rendezvousing with each patrolling USV. These operations require motion-feasible route planning and priority-aware rendezvous sequencing, with the objective of minimizing mission completion time and total tardiness relative to priority-dependent due dates. We propose a framework combining grid-based path planning, surrogate travel-time prediction, and graph-based reinforcement learning. The path planner accounts for ocean currents and approximates turn-rate constraints through multi-stage maneuvers, with path feasibility empirically validated through trajectory-tracking simulations. For rendezvous sequencing, a Feasible Transition Graph (FTG) represents candidate rendezvous events and temporally feasible transitions, enabling a graph neural network to exploit spatiotemporal rendezvous relationships for policy learning. A surrogate predicts travel times from compact current and motion features to avoid repeated path searches during FTG construction and policy training. Evaluation using grid-based path planning shows statistically significant average objective reductions of 10.5%, 3.5%, and 1.5% compared with greedy, MLP-RL, and 2-step lookahead greedy, respectively. The policy achieves these improvements while requiring approximately 1/35 of the latter's computation time. It maintains its advantage over MLP-RL under unseen priority-tier settings, with larger gains as fleet size increases.

How Efficient Is Your Agent? Online Predictive Bounds under Budget Constraints

Roy Maor Lotan, Shai Feldman, Yaniv Romano
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-11-08 LLM & agentic AI

Online predictive bounds on agent time-to-success under budget constraints; evaluation of agents with budget allocation, useful for agent reliability work.

Abstract

We evaluate AI agents in an online setting where tasks arrive sequentially and, before running each task, we construct an upper predictive bound on its \emph{time-to-success}---the number of interactions until the task is completed successfully. Under a limited budget, some runs are stopped early, so their time-to-success is unobserved (right-censored), and the correctness of the bound may be unknown. We introduce \emph{\textbf{PR}edictive Bounds for \textbf{O}nline \textbf{B}udget \textbf{E}fficiency} (\texttt{PROBE}), a method for constructing calibrated upper predictive bounds while adaptively allocating a limited budget across agent tasks to reduce the anticipated coverage deficit. Our method calibrates the bounds online using inverse-probability-weighted feedback together with a correction designed to minimize coverage deficit while remaining unbiased. We prove that \texttt{PROBE} provides a coverage guarantee for any finite time horizon while satisfying the budget constraint. Experiments on software engineering, retrieval-augmented generation, and instruction following show that \texttt{PROBE} attains a coverage rate closer to the target level with tighter predictive bounds than a calibrated baseline.

LEGION: Benchmarking Language Agents on Sparse-Signal Investigation Under Adversarial Noise

Samuel Joseph Amouyal, Ofek Glick, Yaniv Menasherov, Eyal Fisher et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-09 LLM & agentic AI

Benchmark for language agents on sparse-signal investigation with precision/recall evaluation; agentic but cybersecurity-focused, adjacent to agent evaluation interests.

Abstract

Autonomous language-model agents are increasingly deployed on discovery-driven tasks where signal is sparse and most retrieved information is irrelevant. Current benchmarks fail to evaluate this regime due to dense signals, cooperative environments, and ground truth typically being only a few tool calls away. We introduce Legion, a benchmark framework coupling a stochastic enterprise-telemetry simulator (Legion-Sim) with a tiered library of 74 expertly-crafted adversarial scenarios (Legion-Bench) and an evaluation protocol that scores investigation efficiency alongside detection accuracy. Scenarios are injected into freshly simulated companies, allowing independent control over confounder density and event volume. We test five agent architectures on two simulated companies and find that while recall is high (0.82-0.88) precision lags behind (0.61--0.72). They fail to find all of the indicators of compromise of the attack or mistake genuine indicators with attack related ones, which is critical in the context of cybersecurity investigation. Failures are consistent across reasoning tiers and persist across confounder density and event volume. We release Legion, the scenario library, performance on common agent architectures, and pipeline for reproducibility and extension.

Learning to Rank Propagators in Constraint Programming

Matija Lojović, Mladen Nikolić
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-11-08 AI for optimisation

Learns instance-dependent propagator priorities for constraint programming solvers via learning-to-rank; ML-guided solver heuristics.

Abstract

The order in which propagators are executed can substantially affect the performance of constraint programming solvers, yet propagator priorities are typically determined by fixed, manually designed heuristics. In this work, we explore learning instance-dependent propagator priorities from static problem features. We formulate the task as a learning-to-rank problem, addressing the challenge that the quality of a priority assignment can be measured through solver runtime, while the desired priorities of individual propagators are not directly observable. A self-attention-based model jointly scores the propagators present in an instance and is trained using pairwise preferences derived from solver runtimes measured under randomized propagator priority assignments, with a Plackett-Luce-based ranking objective. Predicted rankings are mapped to the discrete priority levels of the Choco solver before search begins, without modifying its propagation or search procedures. We evaluate the approach on XCSP3 instances and demonstrate that propagator ordering can have a substantial impact on solving time. On a test set of 698 instances, picked out as most difficult by problem class, the learned priorities increase the number of solved instances from 634 to 654, reduce median solver runtime by 27.93%, and reduce aggregate penalized runtime (PAR1) by 12.65% compared with Choco's default priorities. We further observe that improvements vary across problem classes and show that a simple policy selecting between learned and default priorities further increases the number of solved instances. These results demonstrate the potential of learning instance-dependent propagator priorities to improve constraint programming performance, particularly on difficult instances.

Treating the Model as an Untrusted Component: Deterministic Policy Enforcement, Human Approval and Taint Tracking Against Prompt Injection in Tool-Using Agents, with Validation on Open-Weights Models

Mustafa S. Aljumaily, Nawar S. Alseelawi, Raghad Alshabandar
arXiv arXiv cs.CR2026-11-08 LLM & agentic AI

Evaluates deterministic reference monitors and taint tracking against prompt injection in tool-using agents; relevant to agent operational safety but not optimisation or transport.

Abstract

Tool-using LLM agents act on instructions embedded in the data they read, so prompt injection is a structural rather than a prompt-wording problem. We compare in-prompt defences (hardened prompts, spotlighting, LLM detectors) with deterministic enforcement outside the model (four reference monitors of increasing strength), human approval and taint tracking, judged by one effect-based oracle independent of every defence in an executable environment of 13 tasks and 12 tools, with argument-fuzzing attackers and severity-weighted harm. In an evaluation with real open-weights models (Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B; 156 attack episodes per defence and model), the models proposed the attacker's call in 64% and 84% of attack episodes behind the task-bound monitor, which admitted 1.3% and 7.7%; guardrail effects depended on the model (a prompt guardrail moved obedience from 67% to 65% and from 86% to 55%, spotlighting plus a detector to 12% and 0%) against static attacks. Simulation with an assumed model covers what no real run did: under the assumed erosion law a persistent adaptive attacker or a compromised model drives every guardrail stack to 100.0% attack success, whereas the task-bound monitor holds severe actions at 0.0%; its residual (harm 0.69/10) falls by 62% under taint-triggered human review, and a human layer without a deterministic monitor fails against approval spamming unless each episode has a deterministic approval budget. Implementation quality matters: a naive monitor admits 18 of 33 effective attack variants, a hardened one 6 and a capability-bound one 1. The real-model evidence is limited to two open-weights models and static attacks, the human-approval and taint results are simulation-only, and the larger model's detector stack contradicts the assumed erosion law. Code, data, a two-rater oracle audit and a notebook for repeating the real-model evaluation are provided.
