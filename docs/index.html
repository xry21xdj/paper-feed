Marginal-Cost Clustering for Courier Dispatch: A Parallel Decomposition for Pickup and Delivery with Time Windows and Fleet Sizing

SangWoo Park, Elson Cibaku, Sanchoy Das
arXiv arXiv eess.SY2026-11-23 E-bus scheduling & charging

Pickup-and-delivery routing with time windows and fleet sizing via clustering and exact labelling, close to dispatch and decomposition methods the researcher uses.

Abstract

Courier dispatch poses a pickup and delivery problem with time windows in which requests are batched, windows are soft, couriers start where they are and take open routes, and a fixed dispatch charge makes fleet size a decision. We contribute to the cluster-first, route-second family a grouping criterion that scores a request by its marginal cost, the travel and lateness it adds to the route a courier already plans, rather than by its distance to a centroid, one point standing for a whole workload in space and time. The marginal cost instead knows where a courier is, when it becomes free and what it already carries. Each pass, we hold the marginal costs fixed while we solve one integer program that assigns every request to a courier and chooses which to open, then improve its solution by a descent that relocates and swaps requests between couriers. We then route each dispatched courier exactly. That subproblem is naturally a mixed-integer program, and we solve it by label setting over visited sets, which we prove optimal and cheap enough to run every pass. On instances we generate and on adapted Li & Lim benchmark batches, we beat the best of six published clustering rules by 29% as published and by 12% once those rules carry the same descent. Against an adaptive large neighbourhood search at matched wall-clock we are ahead on the generated instances and level on the benchmark, and we handle 1000 requests and 300 couriers in about a minute.

AutoSECO: Automated Skill Evolution for Cross-Problem Combinatorial Optimization with General-Purpose LLM Agents

Zirui Zhu, Cong Zhang, Ni Zhang, Kuan Xu et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-23 LLM & agentic AI

General-purpose LLM agents evolve reusable skills to develop solvers for routing and scheduling problems, relevant to agentic optimisation pipelines.

Abstract

General-purpose large language model (LLM) agents can develop complete solvers for combinatorial optimization problems. Their specialization, however, often relies on workflows engineered for individual problems, limiting the reuse of development expertise. We propose AutoSECO, an automated skill evolution method that learns reusable solver-development guidance from experience. The challenge is that external evaluation measures the completed solver, while the skill guides decisions during development. AutoSECO connects this feedback to guidance deficiencies by tracing performance shortfalls through solver code and recorded development decisions. Revisions are expressed as general instructions and retained only if a fresh solver developed under the revised skill achieves a lower mean evaluation gap. Evolved solely on the traveling salesperson problem (TSP), the skill guides fresh agents in developing solvers for capacitated vehicle routing, job-shop scheduling, and three-dimensional bin packing without further skill evolution. On TSP, the final mean benchmark gap is 0.52\%, compared with 0.98\% for the skill-evolution baseline SkillOpt. Across all four problems, the evolved skill achieves better aggregate results than no skill guidance, the initial skill, and SkillOpt. The same frozen skill also supports solver development under alternative model--harness configurations. These findings suggest that solver-development experience can be retained as reusable expertise for general-purpose LLM agents.

Revisiting Learning to Delegate: LLM Discovered Heuristics for Large-Scale Vehicle Routing

Zhengyang Wang, Renfei Tan, Shuze Daniel Liu, David Simchi-Levi
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-23 AI for optimisation

LLM-designed heuristics select subproblems for large-scale CVRP, a routing optimisation method the researcher would want to read on LLM-driven heuristic design.

Abstract

Efficient vehicle routing is critical to logistics and transportation, yet solving large-scale capacitated vehicle routing problems (CVRP) remains computationally challenging due to their combinatorial complexity. One approach to solve large-scale vehicle routing optimization is to identify promising subproblems and then optimize them using solvers. In this paper, we propose a framework of automatic heuristic design (AHD) in which a large language model (LLM) autonomously refines heuristics for selecting subproblems, enabling effective optimization of large-scale CVRP instances. Compared to prior approaches that train task-specific neural networks to select among heuristically constructed subproblems, our method discovers generalizable, interpretable LLM-generated heuristics to directly construct subproblems. Experiments show that our approach achieves comparable in-distribution performance while outperforming neural network-based policies on out-of-distribution instances with large-scale problems.

Service location flexibility in dynamic vehicle routing

Gérard P. Cachon, Bora Cetin, Sophie H. Yu
arXiv arXiv math.OC2026-11-23 E-bus scheduling & charging

Dynamic vehicle routing with service location flexibility via lockers; relevant routing theory for dispatch of a single vehicle.

Abstract

Parcel lockers and delivery robots let a vehicle serve customers at nearby locations. We study this service location flexibility in the dynamic traveling repairman problem, where a single vehicle may serve each demand anywhere within a radius $r$ of its location. Arrivals form a Poisson process, demand locations are independent and uniform in the unit square, and on-site service times are i.i.d. with uniformly bounded second moments, where the radius $r$ may vary across systems as the on-site load $ρ$ tends to one. In this heavy-traffic regime, the optimal mean system time is of order $(1-ρ)^{-1}(1+\min\{1/r,1/(1-ρ)\})$, compared with $(1-ρ)^{-2}$ without service location flexibility [BvR91, PFB11]. Thus, flexibility reduces the order of optimal mean system time when $r$ stays fixed or shrinks more slowly than $1-ρ$. Moreover, when $r$ is on the order of $1-ρ$ or larger, the vehicle can attain the optimal order by repeating a fixed covering route that passes within $r$ of every location. For example, the vehicle can follow a fixed grid route through parcel lockers, with customers in each square cell sharing the locker at its center.

AGL-SKILL: Coding Harnesses as Agentic Optimizers

Ryan Tsui, Yuge Zhang, Yuqing Yang
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-24 LLM & agentic AI

Coding agents used as optimisers of agent skills via score-driven search; relevant to LLM agents that optimise, though not transport specific.

Abstract

Frontier models are increasingly used through harnesses as agents, with growing autonomy to do useful work. Agent performance can often be enhanced by the use of skills: reusable workflows, code, and prompts that provide task-related information or guide the behaviour of the model. Skills, being textual in nature, present themselves as another editable surface to be optimized; current methods have improved on handcrafting and one-shot generation by treating skill development as an iterative process, using score-driven search to tweak and improve performance. In this paper, we demonstrate the efficacy of the agents themselves as optimizers: across five benchmarks, a coding agent without guidance substantially improves every target and outperforms published skill-optimization methods. More broadly, this shows the capability of agents to do such work and to refine their own behaviour to provide task-specific context, as long as they are given a metric to measure against.

Agent Recovery Gone Wrong: Measuring Harm Beyond Duplicate Calls

Artem Trofimov, Boris Novikov
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-23 LLM & agentic AI

Measures harmful recovery behaviour of code-executing LLM agents after partial failures; relevant to agent reliability in operational settings.

Abstract

Code-executing LLM agents submit blocks of code that issue several API writes. If a block fails after some of its writes have taken effect, the agent rewrites the block and continues. It is unknown how often such a recovery harms the external state when the failure occurs naturally in the code written by the agent. In this paper, we measure it on recorded trajectories of AppWorld agents. In a stratified random sample of 200 candidate episodes across ten models, 145 are partial executions confirmed by the database state. In the sample, 30 of 143 confirmed partial executions with a determinable outcome are followed by a harmful recovery, and its violation remains at the end of the run, which gives a stratification-weighted estimate of 24.6%. Two thirds of the harmful recoveries duplicate an effect, and the rest delete user data or make a wrong first-time write, e.g., an order of a wrong product. A rule that searches for repeated calls finds 75% of duplications but only 41% of all harm. We also examine two sources of operation identity for an idempotency contract. In a small pilot (17 episodes, one local model), identifiers supplied by the model do not detectably reduce harm, because the model issues a new identifier for the same operation. Identity derived from the call content, evaluated counterfactually on the sample, would have matched every harmful write in 14 to 18 of the 30 harmful recoveries, but in none of those with a wrong first-time write, and it would also have matched writes required by the task in 30 episodes. Based on these results, we outline design requirements for safer agent recovery.

Agentic AI for Intent Management and Control in Autonomous Communication Networks

Burak Demirel, Yu Wang, Pablo Soldati
arXiv arXiv cs.LG2026-11-23 LLM & agentic AI

Triadic agentic framework with LLM interpreter, Bayesian optimisation and RL controller for network intents; close to agentic optimisation and operational decisions.

Abstract

Artificial intelligence (AI)-driven automation will help future mobile networks translate high-level network intents into concrete network optimization or control actions. However, existing approaches often focus on intent management and orchestration, leaving the adaptation of underlying network optimization and control functions largely unaddressed. This article proposes a triadic agentic AI framework that co-designs intent management and intent fulfillment through three agents: an interpreter, powered by a language model, that translates and refines intents into machine-readable optimization templates; an optimizer that adapts controller policies to intent specifications; and a controller that performs fast decision-making. We discuss the design space of each agent and how the workflow can be embedded across network architectural domains. Finally, we develop a concrete realization combining a language-model interpreter with Bayesian optimization for the optimizer and multi-objective reinforcement learning for the controller, and evaluate it in a radio resource management case study for differentiated connectivity. Results demonstrate how the agentic AI framework can connect high-level intents with runtime network control, showing adaptability of intent specifications and control policies to service requirements and changing network conditions.

Bridging Continuous-Time MAPF and Industrial AGV Fleet Coordination with Lazy Priority-Based Search

Federico Signorile, Raffaele Carli, Alessandro Bertogli, Roberto Olmi et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.RO2026-11-23 AI for optimisation

Continuous-time priority-based MAPF for industrial AGV fleets with a lazy search algorithm; close to fleet scheduling and coordination methods.

Abstract

Continuous-time multi-agent path finding on roadmaps (MAPF$_R$) offers a principled way to coordinate robot fleets with geometric collision avoidance, yet its deployment on industrial automated guided vehicle (AGV) infrastructures remains challenging, as highly connected and redundant roadmaps amplify both safe-interval reasoning and multi-agent interaction handling. To bridge this gap, we formalize AGV-MAPF$_R$-CTC, a configuration-space variant of MAPF$_R$ with precomputed continuous-time conflicts that enables the reuse of MAPF$_R$ solvers for AGV fleet coordination with minimal changes. We then revisit continuous-time priority-based search (CPBS), identifying the boundary conditions required by its safe interval path planning (SIPP) low-level planner under priority-induced constraints and enabling earlier solution detection. As a central contribution, we analyze the structure of the priority search space, clarifying which agents are affected by each priority refinement and when replanning can be avoided. Building on this analysis, we introduce a novel algorithm, Lazy-CPBS, which materializes high-level nodes lazily: a child's plans are computed only when it is expanded, so siblings that are never expanded cost no low-level search. Experiments on standard MAPF benchmarks and on industrial AGV roadmaps imported from a proprietary digital-twin simulator show that CPBS-based solvers remain effective well beyond the agent counts reachable by optimal MAPF$_R$ solvers, while Lazy-CPBS substantially reduces CPBS overhead and offers the most consistent robustness/runtime trade-off across industrial layouts.

ComputeWise: Measuring and Fixing GPU Waste by AI Research Agents

Egor Pakhomov, Nina Pakhomova, Anurag Koul, Shafiq Joty et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-23 LLM & agentic AI

Agents running GPU-based AI research experiments with a simulated GPU replay; agents running simulation pipelines and reducing wasted compute, a loose match to the researcher's agent-simulation interest.

Abstract

AI agents are given GPUs to do AI research on their own. Do they use those GPUs well? A busy GPU does not tell us, because it can be busy with work the answer did not need. This matters, because the tokens the agent spends cost almost nothing next to the GPUs its experiments run on, so using those GPUs better has a huge impact. So we built ComputeWise, an open benchmark of research tasks on which this waste can be measured and reduced. For it we developed our own simulated GPU, which replays recorded runs to the agent's code. With no real GPU needed, we ran hundreds of experiments, far more than would be reasonable on real GPUs. With no help, the agents put only 52% of their GPU time into work the answer needed. Another 22% went to work it did not need, and 26% sat idle. Asking the agent to finish the work faster, telling it to keep every GPU busy, or giving it a job scheduler does not fix this. So we built an open-source harness, part prompting and part deterministic code. Across five models, it removed over 80% of the extra time to the answer.

Congestion-Aware Adaptive Routing for LLM Classification

Yuan Guo, Stefanus Jasin, Chen-An Lin
arXiv arXiv math.OC2026-11-23 AI for optimisation

Congestion-aware routing of queries across LLMs via column generation and convex optimisation, closely matching the researcher's OR methods.

Abstract

Organizations use large language models (LLMs) to classify jobs that differ in uncertainty and the cost of errors. Querying additional models may improve accuracy, but it also incurs fees and increases congestion at shared servers. We study congestion-aware adaptive routing (CAAR) policies that adapt model selection and stopping decisions to observed outputs while accounting for the steady-state congestion they generate. The objective is to minimize the long-run average cost of misclassification penalties, query fees, and holding costs. We can represent CAAR policies as a mixture of decision trees, and derive exact holding costs for first-come-first-served servers with exponential service times and processor-sharing servers with general service times. We formulate a convex optimization problem with unique optimal model loads. Congestion prices capture the marginal system-wide holding costs of additional queries and guide routing and stopping decisions through a Bayesian dynamic program. We characterize optimal stopping decisions and identify models whose information does not justify their cost. A column-generation algorithm computes optimal CAAR policies and provides an explicit optimality-gap bound. We also bound the additional cost of limiting cascade depth relative to full-depth CAAR. Finally, under a common query-depth limit, cascades with a predetermined model order can be arbitrarily more costly than optimal CAAR, even when the order is randomized and stopping decisions adapt to observed outputs. Numerical experiments distinguish the benefits of adaptive model selection and congestion-aware design.

From Rule-based to Agentic: LLM Agent based Real-time Control of Urban Drainage Systems for Flooding and Sewer Overflow Mitigation

Wenchong Tian, Shixun Li, Ruozhou Lin, Peng Ye et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-24 Transport-grid integration

Multi-agent LLM real-time control of urban drainage, a sibling of operational infrastructure control with agentic decision making under disruption.

Abstract

Real-time control (RTC) for urban drainage systems (UDS) remains hampered by sparse in-network sensing. While heterogeneous multi-modal meteorological early warnings often contain decision-critical information for UDS RTC, none of the existing approaches can exploit such externally-sourced information within the control workflow. Here we introduce AgenticAI-Control, an agentic artificial intelligence framework able to assimilate multi-source meteorological early warnings in RTC. Two coupled large language model agents are integrated to generate control strategies by retrieving meteorological early-warnings and fusing them with domain engineering expertise. Validated via simulation studies of a combined sewer catchment in eastern China across 10 real-world rainfall events, the AgenticAI-Control achieved 69.1-90.6% reduction in aggregated flood and overflow volumes compared to the rule-based baseline control. Rigorously standardized prompt engineering and modular workflow constraints substantially compress performance disparities among four distinct LLMs. Our work establishes AgenticAI as a viable tool to unify multi-source meteorological monitoring and practitioner domain knowledge, surmounting longstanding monitoring limitations of UDS RTC and delivering cost-efficient, robust intelligent control solutions for sensor-sparse sewer infrastructures.

Learning What to Change: State-Verified Reinforcement Learning for Educational Agents

Shengyao Wang, Siyuan Su, Siyu Song, Mengsong Wu et al.
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-23 LLM & agentic AI

Reinforcement learning for stateful LLM tutoring agents with verifiable state checks; relevant agentic reliability and tool-use evaluation, though not transport or optimisation.

Abstract

Persistent tutoring agents do more than answer questions: they manage course files, practice records, and learner profiles that govern future lessons. In such stateful settings, knowing what to teach does not guarantee knowing what to change. We identify a pervasive failure mode, content-state decoupling, where agents deliver pedagogically sound responses while silently corrupting workspace state through unwarranted updates, missed prerequisites, or unintended side effects. Text-level judges overlook these state errors, whereas isolated tool-call checks break down because whether a write action is valid depends jointly on the learner request, prior state, and execution order. We propose State-Verified Reinforcement Learning, which supervises online policy optimization directly through verifiable workspace transitions. Within isolated sandboxes that snapshot state around every tool call, a deterministic verifier audits tool effects, workflow ordering, and native auto-sync side effects, pairing graded partial credit with a hard gate that zeroes episode reward upon any critical violation. Whenever no critical violation occurs, an evidence-grounded judge scores actual saved artifacts rather than agent claims. We pair 1,000 verifiable training instances with an independently authored, human-reviewed evaluation benchmark of 60 tasks spanning 11 educational categories and 16 native tools. After online GRPO training, Qwen3.8-27B raises category-weighted strict success on our benchmark from 51.47% to 80.74% (+29.27 points), cutting content-pass-only execution failures from 45.6% to 13.7% and required state-check failures from 63 to 1 across 180 trials. The resulting policy outperforms seven reference baselines by over 20 points while reducing output tokens by 17-39% across seven of eight external benchmarks without regressing on general reasoning or tool use.

Off-the-Shelf Autonomous Agents for Game Playtesting

Ayush Maniar, Eryk Banatt
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-23 LLM & agentic AI

Off-the-shelf LM agent that autonomously plays and solves a 3D game over many turns; agentic planning and exploration relevant to agent evaluation.

Abstract

In playtesting games, a human operator is typically required to test most gameplay elements. In this work we present Buddy, an autonomous game solving agent which can solve long time horizon objectives in an unseen 3D role-playing game using off-the-shelf language model systems. Buddy will take actions, receive feedback on those actions, and pursue a goal across many turns. No written walkthrough or model training of any kind is required, and we show that this paradigm holds in a novel environment where the agent must discover solutions via exploration rather than recalling them from pretraining. We demonstrate three downstream capabilities: game solving with no provided walkthrough, logging bug tickets for encountered difficulties, and conversion of successful agent traces into scripted hierarchical task networks for regression testing. Unlike existing playtesting solutions, our framework is fully modular: language models and harnesses can be easily swapped out as capabilities improve over time.

TACT-Bench: Testing Adaptive Conversational Threats against Tool-Use Agents

Ilia Hashemi Rad, Feijie Wu, Haoyu Wang, Jing Gao
arXiv arXiv cs.AI2026-11-24 LLM & agentic AI

Benchmark testing whether policy-governed tool-use agents resist adaptive attacks while still completing benign work; relevant to agent reliability, though not optimisation.

Abstract

The growing autonomy of AI agents in using external tools and making decisions has created an urgent need for comprehensive safety benchmarks. Existing benchmarks test compromised tool channels or adaptive multi-turn attacks, though they may not capture the unique capabilities of policy-governed tool-use agents, which must resist unauthorized requests without abandoning the legitimate work embedded in the same interaction. To fill this gap, we introduce TACT-Bench, a stateful benchmark built around scenario-specific stories, personas, manipulation tactics, and response-adaptive escalation. Each adversarial task targets an action explicitly prohibited by the policy provided to the assistant, while the policy remains hidden from the attacking user. TACT pairs each adversarial task with at least one benign counterpart, and its adversarial tasks retain authorized ground-truth actions when a compliant resolution remains available. We define these base tasks as TACT-Base. We additionally introduce TACT-TrustPrime, which places an independent benign task before an unrelated attack in the same conversation. We also propose Paired Selective Trustworthiness (PST), which combines explicit safe refusal with demonstrated capability on matched benign tasks, to analyze an agent's overcautiousness and practicality. Averaged across 6 different models as assistants, TACT-Base raises Attack-Induced Failure Rate (AFR) by 17.53% over four compatible attack baselines, and TACT-TrustPrime raises it by a further 17.01%. The two evaluated defense methods reduce AFR by only 8.35%--9.41% on average and do not consistently improve PST, underscoring the need for stronger policy-enforcement methods.

Trustworthy agentic lunar robotics: safety-constrained contingent planning with a foundation-model planner

Bingqi Huang, Bingchuan Wei, Yingkai Cai, Zhaokui Wang
arXiv arXiv cs.RO2026-11-23 LLM & agentic AI

Safety-constrained contingent planning with a foundation-model planner that verifies irreversible actions; relevant to trustworthy agent operational decisions.

Abstract

Sustained lunar-base operations require robots that restore equipment and deliver cargo under conditions unforeseen before deployment. Flown space autonomy executes pre-validated procedures and enters a safe hold outside their scope, whereas foundation models synthesize plans on site that cannot be verified before execution. We formulate these operations as safety-constrained contingent planning with irreversible actions, erroneous records, three-valued physical evidence, and costly, deadline-constrained sensing. We propose asymmetric knowledge-level replanning (AKR), which treats the planner as an untrusted black box: reversible actions run on the planner's assumptions, and irreversible actions run only after a minimum-cost sensing cover verifies every precondition. Under sound measurements, no irreversible action executes on a false precondition for any planner or record, the sensing cost before an irreversible action is bounded below for every sound knowledge-based policy, and execution with a complete planner ends in a verified goal or a certified hold. AKR executed no hazardous action in 21,000 Monte Carlo trials with sound observations or in 58 full-stack runs; the FF-Replan acting rule executed one in 7% of those trials. The median AKR run took 1.10 times the fastest run on the same mission, against 1.50 under the K-replanner acting rule. AKR completed 20 of 21 contingency and sequential-fault runs with an unchanged task model; the original PLEXIL and EUROPA$\rightarrow$PLEXIL procedures completed none.
